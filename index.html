The difference

Most calculators give you one number and hide how they got it.

A single "max offer" is a guess wearing a suit. The exit you choose changes the price by tens of thousands on the same house — so the sheet prices every exit, ranks them against the seller's situation, and never rounds a hole in the data into a number.

01

Six prices, not one

Wholesale, flip, wholetail, buy-and-hold, BRRRR, subject-to, novation. The same house is worth a different maximum under each, and the gap between the best and the worst is usually the whole margin.

02

The seller is an input

An estate wants certainty and a date. Somebody behind on payments wants the loan to stop. The sheet re-ranks on what you know about the person, not just the property — because that is what actually decides which exit closes.

03

It refuses to guess

Every figure is marked entered, estimated, or needed. Estimates visibly widen the range they feed. Exits missing a number refuse to price and say which number they want.

How it stays honest

Three states, and the sheet never mixes them up.

Entered

A fact you typed. The sheet prices it as given and takes responsibility for nothing else.

Estimate

Counted in full — and it widens every range it touches, by an amount that depends on where it came from. A rule of thumb widens less than a ZIP-level median.

Needed

Missing. Exits that depend on it refuse to price rather than invent it, and name the figure they are waiting for.

The eight exits

There are only eight ways out of a property deal.

Every one of them has a different maximum price on the same house — often tens of thousands apart. Picking the wrong one is not a rounding error, it is the whole deal. The desk prices all eight and tells you which fits the person you are actually talking to.

Who it is for

Four jobs, one sheet, four different answers.

The exit you are already committed to is usually the one costing you the deal. The same inputs answer all four questions at once.

Wholesaling

Often there is no asking price at all — the seller wants your number first. Say so, and the sheet ranks by what each exit lets you pay, then hands you an opening figure and what your terms are worth against it.

Flipping

Your max buy, the 70% rule beside it, and a grid showing what happens to the profit when the ARV is 10% light and the repairs run 40% over. The gap is your margin for being wrong.

Holding

The price a 1.25 DSCR actually allows at today's rate and LTV — the number the lender underlines — with every assumption on a slider you can move.

Creative

Subject-to and novation priced properly, including the ones the sheet refuses: a retail listing on a house that is not retail yet gets declined, with the reason.

What it replaces

The spreadsheet you rebuilt three times and still do not trust.
