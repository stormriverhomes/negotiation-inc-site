What the plan actually buys

Nine things that give you the afternoon back.

Underwriting a house takes about forty minutes, and roughly thirty-two of them are typing things a computer already knows. These are the thirty-two.

Underwriter

Read the condition from the photos

Drop the forty pictures you took walking the house. Every line comes back filled in — roof, mechanicals, each bath, the drive — with a number on each and the photo it came from. You correct the three it read wrong instead of typing seventeen.

Saves ~20 minutes per property, and catches the line you forgot
Underwriter

Comps pulled, scored and adjusted

Type the address; recent sales arrive on the workbench already adjusted for size, beds, baths, time and condition, each with a comparability score and the reasons for it. It runs on our key, so there is no vendor account to open and nothing to paste — forty pulls a month on Underwriter, a hundred and twenty on The Office, counted and shown. If you would rather it ran on your own RentCast key, paste it in and it does: kept in your browser, never sent to us, billed to you at their price. That is also how the free desk pulls comps. Either way it is still not an AVM: the sales arrive unscored, the range is drawn from the ones you keep, and the ARV is the one you set.

Saves ~10 minutes, and the band gets narrower with every comp
Solo

The lender packet, in one click

One PDF with your name on it: the property, the comps you used, the condition read, every exit priced, and the arithmetic behind the number you are asking them to fund. Nothing hand-assembled, nothing missing. The same document works on a private lender, a partner, or a seller who wants to know how you got there.

Saves an hour of spreadsheet-into-Word, per deal you fund
Solo

A portfolio that remembers

Every property you priced, side by side, with its spread, its best exit and the day you ran it. Open it on the phone in the driveway and on the laptop that evening and it is the same sheet. The eleven deals you looked at last week stop being eleven browser tabs you closed.

The one that compounds — last month's comps are this month's context
Solo

Which of these two?

Nobody has one house. Put two sheets side by side and the same engine prices both — best exit, ceiling, room, spread, confidence — with one set of assumptions driving both. Then the part nothing else does: move the rate, the hold, the target profit across their whole range and it tells you where the winner changes. "This one, unless rates pass 9.4%" is an answer. "Both look fine" is not.

The Tuesday question — not "is this a deal" but "is this the deal"

The letter, and the text message

The sheet already knows which exit won, what the terms are worth in dollars, and what to concede first. It writes the offer letter and the message that goes with it — in the seller's language, not yours — with the number, the close date and the two concessions that cost you least, ready to send or to edit.

The gap most people lose the deal in: knowing the number, fumbling the ask

A bid that lands eleven thousand under because it does not mention the panel is not eleven thousand cheaper; it is the same job with a change order in it.

Before you ask

The things people ask us.

How much can I really do for free?

Close a deal. Three comps draw a real range, the three rehab profiles price a rehab honestly, and every exit prices with its arithmetic shown. The free tier is narrower, not weaker — what a plan buys is precision and speed, never permission.

Why $129 when there are $20 deal calculators?

Because they are calculators and you type into them. Solo, at $39, is the honest comparison — and it does more than any of them. What $129 buys is the part that is not arithmetic: the comps arrive already scored, the condition comes off your photographs, and the letter writes itself. That is a data-and-AI product, and every other one of those in this category starts at $99 and only gives you leads.

Which one should I be on?

If you price a house every few months, free genuinely closes deals. If you have a model you argue with and files you come back to, Solo. If you are underwriting weekly and the forty minutes of typing is the actual cost, Underwriter. If two people price and the numbers have to agree, The Office. Move up when something annoys you, not before.

Do you sell my data?

There is nothing to sell. The free desk never sends a property anywhere — the arithmetic runs in your browser. A workspace stores your sheets so they follow you, and that is the only thing it stores.

Where do the comps come from?

Public records and licensed feeds, never scraped from a portal that forbids it. On Underwriter and The Office the pull runs on our RentCast key and is metered — forty a month, or a hundred and twenty. You can also paste in your own key, on any tier including the free desk, in which case the lookups are billed to you by RentCast and we never see the key or the results. Whichever way they arrive, they arrive unscored.

What happens when the trial ends?

Day fifteen is the first charge, and we tell you on day eleven. Cancel before then and you land on the free tier — nothing is deleted, no sheet is locked, and every number you already worked out stays readable. You lose the parts a professional keeps, not your work.

Can I cancel?

In one click, from the workspace, without an email to anybody. The month you paid for finishes and then it stops.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is arithmetic with its working shown, so you can check it. Every estimate is marked as one, and the sheet refuses to price an exit it cannot justify rather than guessing.

Price one, and decide after.

The desk is open, free, and needs nothing from you — no card, no account, no expiry. Price a house on it first. If it gives you the afternoon back, the plans will still be here.
